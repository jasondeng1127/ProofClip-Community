# ProofClip Community distribution kit

Use this kit to introduce the Community edition to people who research with
long-form web sources. Keep every post factual about the self-hosted product
boundary and its explicit-delivery model.

![ProofClip Community social preview: research capture modes and a real side-panel crop](assets/social-preview.png)

## Who it is for

Start with literature, academic, archival, policy, and document researchers
who need to preserve a quotation, figure, or page context without leaving the
source they are reading. The Community edition suits people who want to
self-deploy and operate their own environment.

## Short post

> ProofClip Community is a self-hosted research workbench for keeping web
> evidence close to the reading that produced it. Capture a quotation with
> `Alt+1`, a figure with `Alt+2`, or a full page with `Alt+3`; then open the
> saved local Archive record to add your own research note. Delivery to Notion
> is explicit—there is no background or automatic sync. The Community edition
> is available for people who want to self-deploy: [GitHub repository](https://github.com/jasondeng1127/ProofClip-Community).

## Longer post

> Research often breaks when a useful passage, chart, or page needs to be
> copied into a note system before the thought is finished. ProofClip
> Community keeps that step close to the source: use `Alt+1` for a selection,
> `Alt+2` for an image area, or `Alt+3` for the full page. Evidence defaults to
> the local Archive, where it can be reviewed with a personal note, projects,
> tags, search, and editable templates. When a record belongs in Notion, the
> user explicitly chooses delivery; there is no background or automatic sync.
>
> This is the self-deployable Community edition for users who want to operate
> their own Cloudflare Worker/D1 and Notion OAuth environment. Start with the
> [Community release](https://github.com/jasondeng1127/ProofClip-Community/releases/tag/v0.7.0-community), then read the [deployment guide](../deploy/README.md).

## Suggested posting sequence

1. Publish the release link and the short post on GitHub Discussions or a
   relevant developer/research-tools community.
2. Share the longer post, the social preview, and the Community release in one
   literature, academic-research, or Notion-focused community where
   self-hosting is relevant.
3. Reply only to concrete deployment, research-workflow, or evidence-integrity
   questions. Do not post the same text repeatedly across unrelated groups.

## Seven-day validation

| Item | Operating rule |
| --- | --- |
| Goal | Find out whether self-hosted research evidence capture attracts real deployers or high-quality research-workflow feedback. |
| Core assumption | Researchers value keeping page context and personal notes together enough to try a self-deployed tool. |
| Cost and time ceiling | No paid promotion; at most three targeted posts and three hours in 7 days. |
| Measurement | Deployment-guide visits, substantive GitHub Discussions/issues, reported deployments, and commercial-edition enquiries. |
| Successful signal | At least 10 deployment-guide visits with at least 3 substantive questions or reports, and at least 1 reported deployment or qualified commercial enquiry. |
| Stop signal | Only generic reactions, no substantive questions, and no deployer or research-workflow feedback after 7 days. Stop broad sharing and interview three target researchers before changing the message. |
| Next step | If the successful signal appears, publish one anonymized research workflow example; otherwise revise the research problem statement from interviews. |
